# Maps Decision Atlas

This is the **Maps product-intent authority** for owner-approved, consequential viewer and application-developer capabilities. It complements, rather than replaces, the existing [engine roadmap](engine-roadmap.md), [Map-Scoped Timeline reference](timeline.md), repository `AGENTS.md`, and accepted ADRs. Engine contracts and implementation sequencing remain in their existing owners.

## Rules

- Semantic entries are only **Decision** and **Question**. Headings organize capabilities.
- Decisions record product intent, **not** proof of implementation. Questions are unresolved choices, **not** automatically blockers.
- IDs never change or disappear. When answering a Question, retain it and append a Decision that explicitly `Answers <ID>`. When revising a Decision, preserve the original and append an explicit `Supersedes <ID>` Decision.
- Keep reusable Maps geographic/viewer semantics here. Consumers such as `interactive-videos` own episode narratives, historical assertions, actual period datasets, translations, and integration-specific acceptance. GitHub issues track bounded work; tests and existing ADRs own engine and data-architecture invariants.

## 1. Historical Map Views

- **1.1 Interactive historical maps combine selectable locations with time-aware geography.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13, cross-repository steering from [interactive-videos Decision 3.18](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/interactive-videos/pull/58).  
  A consuming application can show a **pannable, zoomable Map View**, let viewers **select locations and inspect information**, and use a **Map-Scoped Timeline** to select historical dates/epochs. The geographic scene can include **time-varying regional/political boundaries and time-relevant locations/markers** that change together as time changes. Controls and selections should stay coherent when users seek backward and forward. Map presentation must not suggest an unsupported historical boundary is exact; retain source/accuracy/illustrative metadata supplied with the data so the consuming viewer can distinguish sourced versus interpretive geometry. The Map Library supplies reusable view, layer, time and selection behavior; it does **not** supply or certify a universal history-of-the-world boundary dataset or take ownership of the Seven Councils' historical claims.

- **1.2** [question] **Behavior between dated historical snapshots.**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: for a historical Map View whose source contains only discrete dated boundary snapshots, should its selectable timeline (A) hold/snap to the previous *documented* snapshot with the effective source year conspicuously shown, (B) optionally interpolate transitions with a clear **illustrative interpolation** label, or (C) offer only the supported dates and mark intervening time unsupported? This is a reusable viewer contract, not a choice of source data. The current History demo's explicit CShapes-Europe policy in [ADR 0005](adr/0005-use-illustrative-history-demo-data.md) remains unchanged unless separately revised. A concrete consumer can still select exact dated snapshots while this broader rule is unresolved.
  Answered by Decision 1.4; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.3 Historical Map Views can disclose sourced versus reconstructed boundaries.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13, cross-repository steering from [interactive-videos Decision 3.20](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/interactive-videos/pull/58) (**Answers its Question 3.19, option B**).  
  A consuming Historical Map View may display application-supplied **approximate or scholarly reconstructed political boundaries** alongside better-supported source-based geometry. Viewers must be able to **distinguish reconstructed from documentary boundaries**, understand the effective source date/period and inspect supplied **attribution, provenance and uncertainty/limitation notes** while panning, zooming, selecting Map Features or changing the Map-Scoped Timeline. Maps owns reusable map-layer presentation, feature selection and disclosure of those distinctions—not their historical interpretation, certainty scoring, source research or translations. When consumers provide no defensible boundary geometry, the Map View must not silently invent it. Do not present illustrative interpolations between dated snapshots as observed facts; the generic date-between-snapshots behavior is separately governed by **Decision 1.4 (Answers Question 1.2, B)** and its snapshot-first default under **1.6**. The existing ADR 0005 post-1816 demo-data policy stays unchanged.

- **1.4 Optional, visibly illustrative interpolation between dated snapshots.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.2 (option B).**  
  Historical Map Views with two documented, dated geographic states may **offer an optional interpolated visual transition** between them, allowing viewers to scrub through intermediate dates rather than only switching between snapshots. At an intermediate date, the interpolated geometry must be **prominently identified as hypothetical/illustrative interpolation**, never as historically attested borders or a scholarly reconstruction specific to that intermediate date. Show the selected date and the **actual dates/provenance of the source snapshots** it bridges, so the viewer can distinguish documentary evidence (or separately documented scholarly reconstructions under Decision 1.3) from computer-generated in-between shapes. A disabled or unavailable interpolation must not be represented as source evidence. Preserve exact supported endpoint states and deterministic seeking backward/forward. Reuse existing Map-Scoped Timeline and geographic transition foundations instead of inventing a second time model or rendering engine. The existing CShapes-Europe History demo explicitly uses hold/snap without morphs under [ADR 0005](adr/0005-use-illustrative-history-demo-data.md); **do not silently change that accepted demo-specific rule**. This decision does not mandate that every historical dataset support interpolation or settle the default on/off presentation for optional interpolation.

- **1.5** [question] **Default presentation of optional interpolation.**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: when a historical dataset supplies dated snapshots and supports the illustrative interpolation of 1.4, should a newly opened view (A) show only the documented snapshots by default, with an explicit viewer control to turn on interpolation; (B) show labeled illustrative interpolation by default, with a control to view only documentary snapshots; or (C) let the consuming application explicitly choose the starting mode for its own historical story, while always allowing viewers to distinguish/choose the modes? Do not infer a universal default from the existing CShapes-Europe History demo, whose separate ADR remains binding.
  Answered by Decision 1.6; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.6 Historical Map Views start with source snapshots, not interpolated boundaries.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.5 (option A).**  
  When a historical Map View offers the optional illustrated interpolation permitted by Decision 1.4, its **initial mode shows only the supplied dated source snapshots**, respecting each snapshot's documentary, scholarly reconstruction or uncertainty labeling under Decision 1.3. It must not draw an **interpolated intermediate boundary by default**. The viewer can explicitly enable the hypothetical/illustrative interpolation and can disable it to return to snapshot-only presentation, without losing the requested historical context or confusing the selected date with the actual source date. While interpolation is enabled, visibly distinguish computed in-between geometry from documented or scholarly reconstructed dated geography and expose both source dates/provenance (1.4). Consumers must not silently turn interpolation on by default. This is a default for Maps historical viewers **where interpolation is available**, not a requirement to provide it on every map. The existing CShapes-Europe History demo remains snap/hold only under ADR 0005 unless that separate accepted policy is explicitly revised.

- **1.7** [question] **Visual behavior when a region's boundaries change between snapshots.**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: when illustrative interpolation is explicitly enabled, should a historical Map View (A) use a smooth shape morph between dated boundaries, even if some unchanged land appears to shift; (B) keep unchanged geographic territory anchored and visualize only land/regions gained or lost, without sliding stable areas; or (C) offer both methods as visibly distinct viewer-selectable modes? The existing keyframe/transition contract in [#173](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/maps/issues/173) and the deferred geography-preserving mode in [#175](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/maps/issues/175) are engineering evidence, not owner approval of a particular visual story. Do not conflate any computed transition with an observed historical border.
  Answered by Decision 1.8; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.8 Historical transitions preserve unchanged geography and show territorial gains and losses.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.7 (option B).**  
  When a viewer explicitly enables the **illustrative historical interpolation** allowed by Decision 1.4 (off by default under 1.6), a changing region must **keep geographically unchanged territory fixed in place** while depicting the portions gained or lost between its two dated source states. Do **not** slide, warp or morph unchanged land simply to animate a border, and do not present any computed intermediate shape as a documented historical boundary. Preserve exact documented endpoint geometries, source-year/provenance and reconstructed-versus-attested distinctions (1.3–1.4). A case the supported transition semantics cannot depict without misleading motion must remain an explicitly labeled snapshot/discrete change rather than silently falling back to an inaccurate smooth morph. Reuse the existing Map-Scoped Timeline (issue #172), geographic operations/transitions (issue #173) and the **geography-preserving region transition** work (issue #175) as the owning foundations; this selected visual behavior makes the geography-preserving capability necessary **for historical interpolation where offered**, but does not block ordinary map parity or require interpolation in the existing CShapes-Europe History demo (ADR 0005). Maps owns this visualization contract; consuming applications own historical source and interpretive claims.

- **1.9** [question] **What should viewers see when historical polities split or merge?**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: for explicitly enabled illustrative historical transitions where regions are replaced by multiple successors or combined into a new polity, should the Map View (A) use a clearly dated discrete switch, with no interpolation across that change; (B) show anchored territorial gains/losses **where trustworthy source data identifies the predecessor/successor relationship**, otherwise use a labeled discrete switch; or (C) animate every split/merge through inferred geometric matches even without supported historical succession? This choice changes the viewer's interpretation of political continuity, not the accuracy of the source dataset itself.
  Answered by Decision 1.10; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.10 Historical polity splits and mergers animate only evidenced succession.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.9 (option B).**  
  If a historical Map View offers optional, explicitly enabled interpolation (1.4, 1.6) and a dated region **splits into successors or merges with others**, it may animate **geography-preserving territorial gains and losses** (1.8) **only when supplied, trustworthy historical evidence explicitly establishes the predecessor/successor relationship**. Identify the source states, relevant dates and provenance of that relationship; do not infer political continuity merely because polygons overlap, touch or have similar shapes. Where a relationship is absent, disputed without adequate grounding, or unsuitable for an accurate anchored transition, display a **clearly dated discrete change** instead of inventing an animated succession. The transition remains an **illustrative depiction**, not footage or proof of exactly how a historical event unfolded; documentary and scholarly reconstruction labels stay distinct from the interpolation label. Maps owns reusable presentation of source-supplied historical identity/lineage and time-controlled geometry; consuming applications own researching and justifying political succession. This is the required behavior **only for supported splits/mergers when historical interpolation is offered**, not an instruction to enable interpolation by default or change the existing CShapes-Europe History demo's snapshot-only policy.

- **1.11** [question] **How should viewers explore competing reconstructions of the same historical territory?**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: when reputable historical sources disagree about a region's boundaries at a date, should a Map View (A) show the consuming application's selected sourced reconstruction and explain the disagreement in attribution/context; (B) let the viewer choose among separately sourced reconstructions, displaying one at a time; or (C) support visual side-by-side or overlaid comparisons of competing sourced reconstructions? All options must avoid blending alternative hypotheses into an apparently authoritative border, preserve date/provenance and respect the consumer's ownership of historical evidence.

  Answered by Decision 1.12; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.12 Use one curated sourced reconstruction, with alternative interpretations explained.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.11 (option A).**  
  The Historical Map View presents **one historical boundary reconstruction chosen by its consuming application**, identifying its source, relevant date and uncertainty rather than implying scholarly consensus. Where credible competing reconstructions exist, the accompanying attributed context explains that alternatives exist and the meaningful disagreement. The application supplies the preferred boundary and explanatory evidence; Maps presents that information and its provenance. A viewer is **not required** to switch among competing boundary geometries or see side-by-side/overlaid alternatives. Where no defensible reconstruction exists, do not invent a polygon. This does not change the historical interpolation, source-date or CShapes-Europe demo policies in Decisions 1.3–1.10 and ADR 0005.

- **1.13** [question] **Selection when changing historical dates.**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: if the viewer is inspecting a historical place or polity and changes to a date when it no longer applies, should the map (A) clear that selection and close its details, (B) retain those details visibly marked as out-of-period while clearing the map selection, or (C) automatically follow an explicitly sourced successor where one exists and otherwise clear the selection? Decision 1.10 establishes when historical continuity may be portrayed; it does not itself require following a successor.
  Answered by Decision 1.14; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.14 Retain inspected historical context without stale map selection.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.13 (option B).**  
  If a viewer is inspecting a Historical Map View place or polity and moves the Map-Scoped Timeline to a date **outside that feature's applicable period**, clear its active geographic selection/highlight, hover and any popup tied to its now-inapplicable map geometry. **Keep the previously opened historical details available** in a separate context surface, visibly labeled **out of period**; identify the date/period to which the details refer and the map's newly selected date, preserving any supplied historical attribution. The retained panel must not suggest that the feature still exists on, is selectable at or occupies its former map location in the new period. Do **not** automatically select a successor (1.10) or rewind the map's date. A new applicable Map Feature selection may replace the retained context, and the viewer can dismiss it. Maps owns the coherent Map Feature selection, date-aware rendering and detached detail presentation; consumers own historical validity ranges, content, sources and translations. This behavior does not change the CShapes-Europe History demo's dated source snapshots under ADR 0005.

- **1.15** [question] **Returning from retained out-of-period details to the historical map.**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: once a viewer has kept the contextual details of a no-longer-active polity while examining a later date (1.14), should (A) the detail panel be purely informational and the viewer return by manually moving the timeline, (B) it offer an explicit **Show at its historical date** action that moves to a supported date and reselects the feature, or (C) show its evidenced historical date/period on a contextual mini-timeline from which viewers can jump to supported dates? All options must keep the current map date and selection unchanged until the viewer explicitly requests navigation; do not conflate historically evidenced feature lifetimes with automatically inferred successor identity.
  Answered by Decision 1.16; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.16 Retained historical details offer a contextual mini-timeline for explicit navigation.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-13. **Answers 1.15 (option C).**  
  When a historical Map Feature has become **out of period** and its details remain open under Decision 1.14, the detached context surface shows a **compact mini-timeline** of that feature's **evidence-backed applicable dates/period**. It lets the viewer select a **supported date** to revisit the feature. A jump occurs **only on explicit viewer action**; until then, the main Map-Scoped Timeline's time, active selection and camera remain unchanged. The mini-timeline is **navigation for the existing Map-Scoped Timeline, not a second independent clock, playback controller or historical timeline engine**. On a valid jump, set the single authoritative map date, restore the same historical feature's selection when applicable and present its current-period context. Dates outside supported evidence, unavailable feature states or unsupported jumps must be identified honestly, not fabricated or represented as valid. Do not automatically select a successor or invent continuous political identity from geometric overlap (1.10). Preserve the original/source date, selected-map date and attribution; the consumer supplies valid historical periods, dates and localized details, while Maps owns selection/time synchronization and the reusable compact timeline control. Preserve ADR 0005's snapshot-only CShapes-Europe History demo policy.

- **1.17** [question] **How should maps label places whose names change over time?**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-13: when a place or polity has different documented historical and modern names, should its map label (A) use the **name appropriate to the selected historical date**, with its modern equivalent available in the details; (B) use a stable **modern recognizable name** on the map, with the period-specific name in details; or (C) display **both historical and modern names** directly on the map where applicable? This choice changes how viewers interpret names while scrubbing history; the application owns sourced naming and translations, and Maps should not invent historical names.
  Answered by Decision 1.18; retained for historical traceability.

- **1.18 Show date-appropriate historical names on the map, with modern equivalents in details.**  
  Status: owner-approved 2026-10-14. **Answers 1.17 (option A).**  
  In a Historical Map View, the **primary on-map label** for a place or polity uses its **documented historical name applicable to the viewer's selected Map-Scoped Timeline date**. Its recognizable **modern equivalent** is available in the place's inspection details, not obligatorily crowded into the map label. When a historically continuous place legitimately changes its name across dated snapshots, its displayed label changes with the map date while its **stable Map Feature identity and selection** are preserved where the supplied evidence supports continuity; a shared name alone never proves political identity or succession (1.10). Do not silently substitute a modern name as though it were attested for an earlier century, invent a date-specific ancient name, or conceal missing/contested naming evidence. The consumer supplies historically sourced names, their applicable periods, modern equivalents, provenance and translations; Maps owns date-aware labeling and accessible detail presentation without acquiring historical-data authority. Retained out-of-period context continues to identify its own dated name and period (1.14–1.16). Original-script versus viewer-language spelling is a separate open Question 1.19. Existing CShapes-Europe demo-data policy (ADR 0005) remains unchanged.

- **1.19** [question] **How should map labels present historical names in different languages and scripts?**  
  Unresolved 2026-10-14: when a documented historical place name was written in Greek, Latin or another language/script, and a consuming experience supports multiple viewer languages, should the primary map label (A) show its historically attested original-language form/script, with a readable transliteration or localized form in details; (B) show the **established period-appropriate transliteration or historical name in the viewer's selected language**, with the attested original form in details; or (C) show both original and selected-language historical forms directly on the map where space allows? In all cases the selected **historical date** governs the name under Decision 1.18; avoid inventing historical spelling or treating a modern replacement as a period name. This changes readability in the Seven Councils' English/German/Spanish experience without choosing who authors translations.

## Evidence and boundaries

- The [existing History demo](https://moritzbrantner.github.io/maps/) already provides year selection and selectable polity geometry for CShapes-Europe-derived scenes at **1816, 1886, 1914, 1939, 1945, 1989 and 2019**, plus an explicitly illustrative WWII control scenario. This is evidence of existing behavior, not proof of support for council-era political boundaries.
- [ADR 0005](adr/0005-use-illustrative-history-demo-data.md) expressly forbids treating that post-1816, noncommercially licensed illustrative demo data as an authoritative historical boundary source or published Maps package data.
- [Issue #172](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/maps/issues/172) tracks first-party Map-Scoped Timeline/temporal parity and [issue #170](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/maps/issues/170) covers mixed Map View interactions, camera and selection. Do not duplicate these foundational engines.
- The consuming [interactive-videos series](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/interactive-videos/pull/58) owns the seven councils (325–787), the correctness and provenance of any era-appropriate historical geography, and the localized council/participant descriptions. Its Decision **3.20 answers Question 3.19 (B)** by allowing clearly qualified scholarly reconstructions in the council content. This authorizes reusable display support here, not a Maps-owned ancient political-boundary dataset.
